Gwanak-gu, Seoul

관악구 · 3 neighborhoods · Learn Korean administrative divisions with maps

About Gwanak-gu

Located southwest of Seoul, with the majestic Mount Gwanak as its backdrop, this autonomous district is a major residential area centered around Bongcheon-dong and Sillim-dong. Home to Seoul National University, it boasts a thriving university-area commercial district and is also well known for Nakseongdae, the birthplace of General Gang Gam-chan, a renowned military commander of the Goryeo Dynasty.

History of Gwanak-gu

During the Three Kingdoms period, the area was ruled in turn by Baekje, Goguryeo, and Silla, and during the Goryeo Dynasty, it was known as Geumju (衿州). In the Joseon Dynasty, it was a rural area belonging to Siheung County and Gwacheon County. Legend has it that Mount Gwanak—one of the Five Great Mountains of Gyeonggi—was considered the ‘Mountain of Fire,’ and a Haetae statue was erected in front of Gwanghwamun Gate to suppress the fire energy emanating from Gyeongbokgung Palace. In 1963, it was incorporated into Seoul and became part of Yeongdeungpo-gu, but in 1973, it was separated and established as Gwanak-gu. In 1975, Seoul National University relocated here, and the area has since grown into the university town it is today.

People & Stories

Nakseongdae was named after a legend that a large star fell from the sky at the time of General Gang Gam-chan’s birth, and the Inheon Festival is held annually to honor his spirit of patriotism. There are also many legends associated with Gang Gam-chan. One story tells that while trying to eliminate a thunderbolt on Mount Gwanak, he accidentally uprooted all the kudzu vines, which is why kudzu vines are rare on this mountain. Another legend, passed down at Yeollyeoam (Rock of the Virtuous Woman) on Mount Gwanak, tells of a woman who, after losing her husband, remained chaste and prayed so fervently that her entire family was turned into fossils.
