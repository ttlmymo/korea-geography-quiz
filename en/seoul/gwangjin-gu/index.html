Gwangjin-gu, Seoul

About Gwangjin-gu

Located in eastern Seoul, this autonomous district is nestled between the Han River and Mount Achasan. It is a vibrant, youthful area home to Children’s Grand Park, Konkuk University, and Sejong University. It is a place where the natural beauty of Ttukseom Han River Park and the Achasan area blends harmoniously with the riverside scenery leading to Walker Hill.

History of Gwangjin-gu

The Achasan area was a battleground during the Three Kingdoms Period, where Goguryeo, Baekje, and Silla fought over the Han River for about 200 years; Achasan Fortress remains as a testament to that era. During the Joseon Dynasty, the Salgoteui Pasture—a national ranch where the royal family’s horses were raised—was located here in Salgoteui Valley, and the area was also used as the king’s hunting ground. The site, which had been occupied by a golf course during the Japanese colonial period, was developed into Children’s Grand Park in 1973, and in 1995, Gwangjin-gu was established after being separated from Seongdong-gu.

People & Stories

Legend has it that the name Achasan originates from an incident during the reign of King Myeongjong, when the renowned fortune-teller Hong Gye-gwan was about to be executed for failing to correctly guess the number of mice in a chest, but the king belatedly realized his mistake, exclaiming, ‘Ah, I was wrong!’ It is said that Yeonghwa Temple on Achasan Mountain was founded by Venerable Uisang during the reign of King Munmu of Silla, and at Gwangnaru, the tradition of the Dragon King Festival (Yangjinsa), where people prayed for safe voyages, remains and continues to this day.
