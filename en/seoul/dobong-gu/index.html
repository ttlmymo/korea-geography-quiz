About Dobong-gu

As the northernmost gateway to Seoul, this is a pleasant residential area where the Jungnang Stream flows against the backdrop of the towering Mount Dobong. It is known as a place where nature, history, and culture harmoniously blend, featuring Dobong Seowon and various hermitages at the foot of Mount Dobong, as well as the Tomb of Prince Yeonsan in Banghak-dong and the Kim Soo-young Literature Museum.

History of Dobong-gu

In ancient times, it was a strategic location successively occupied by Baekje, Goguryeo, and Silla. It was known as Maeseong-gun during the Goguryeo period, Naeso-gun during the Unified Silla period, and Gyeonju (見州) during the Goryeo period, before being incorporated into Yangju. During the Joseon Dynasty, it belonged to Yangju, where place names such as Chang-dong, Dobong, Banghak, and Ssangmun took root. In 1963, it was incorporated into Seoul and became part of Seongbuk-gu. In 1973, Dobong-gu was established as a separate district split from Seongbuk-gu, and subsequently, Nowon-gu was separated in 1988 and Gangbuk-gu in 1995, resulting in the area’s current configuration. For this reason, it is still often grouped together with Gangbuk-gu and Nowon-gu as Seoul’s ‘northeastern region’.

People & Stories

Banghak-dong is home to the tombs of King Yeonsan, who caused two political upheavals before being deposed in a counter-revolution, and his wife, Lady Shin of Geochang; next to them stands a ginkgo tree that has lived for a thousand years and has been designated as Seoul’s No. 1 Protected Tree. On Chosan Mountain in Chang-dong, there are approximately 1,000 tombs of people from various social classes, including those of eunuchs from the Joseon Dynasty, which illustrate the evolution of ancient burial customs and stone artifacts. Additionally, in Ssangmun-dong, the story of the 'Ssangmun' (Double Gate)—a gate erected to honor three generations of filial piety—has been passed down, and this became the origin of the neighborhood’s name.
