Yongsan-gu, Seoul

About Yongsan-gu

This autonomous district is situated along the route connecting downtown Seoul and Gangnam, stretching from the southern slopes of Namsan Mountain to the Han River. It has long served as a strategic hub for military bases and railways, and is an international district home to Itaewon and the embassies of 41 countries, as well as a place where history and culture blend harmoniously, featuring attractions such as the National Museum of Korea, the War Memorial of Korea, and Hyochang Park.

History of Yongsan-gu

In ancient times, it was part of the Mahan Confederacy and early Baekje. After the rivalry among the Three Kingdoms, it became part of Hanyang County during the Unified Silla period and belonged to Yangju during the Goryeo Dynasty. It was such a strategic hub for Han River water transport that it was even considered as a candidate site when the Southern Capital was established. During the Joseon Dynasty, it belonged to the Yongsan-bang, Hangang-bang, and Dunji-bang districts within the ten-ri perimeter of Hanseongbu. Logistics on the Han River flowed through the area centered on the Yongsan River, and transportation routes such as Itaewon and Cheongpa Station were developed. After the opening of the ports, a foreign settlement formed in the Wonhyo-ro area, and following the victory in the Russo-Japanese War, the Japanese Empire established military and railway bases on Sinyeongsan, turning the area into a stronghold for colonial rule and continental aggression. After liberation, the headquarters of the South Korean and U.S. militaries were established there, and recently, with the relocation of the U.S. military base, the creation of Yongsan Park and the development of the International Business District are underway.

People & Stories

Hyochang Park in Hyochang-dong was originally the burial ground of Crown Prince Munhyo, the eldest son of King Jeongjo, but it has now established itself as a sacred site of national spirit where the remains of the three patriots—Yun Bong-gil, Lee Bong-chang, and Baek Jeong-gi—as well as key figures of the Provisional Government, including Baekbeom Kim Gu, are interred. Saenamteo and Dang-gogae in Ichon-dong are holy sites where numerous Catholics, including Father Kim Dae-geon, were martyred, and the Itaewon area is home to stories about place names steeped in the pain of the Imjin War. Additionally, at the General Nam-i Shrine in Yongmun-dong, the legend of Nam-i’s marriage—in which he is said to have revived the daughter of Kwon Ram when he was a child—is preserved, and the General Nam-i Shrine Festival continues as a Seoul Intangible Cultural Heritage.
