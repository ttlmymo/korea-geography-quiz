About Gangdong-gu

Located at the southeastern tip of Seoul, south of the Han River, this autonomous district is an area steeped in history, home to the Amsa-dong Prehistoric Site. It consists primarily of residential areas and boasts a pleasant natural environment, including Ilja Mountain and the banks of the Han River.

History of Gangdong-gu

Gangdong-gu marks the beginning of Seoul’s history. Amsa-dong is home to one of Korea’s most representative archaeological sites of a Neolithic-era settlement, where people lived approximately 6,000 years ago. The site gained recognition in 1925 when the Great Flood of the Year of the Ox eroded the riverbank, exposing comb-patterned pottery. Due to its proximity to Pungnap Fortress—believed to be the capital of the Baekje Kingdom across the Han River—it served as a center of Baekje culture and was also the ancestral home of the Gwangju Lee clan and the scholar Dunchon Lee Jip during the late Goryeo Dynasty. During the Joseon Dynasty, it was a rural area belonging to Gucheon-myeon, Gwangju-bu, and served as a timber collection point where rafts from Gangwon Province would stop before traveling down to Mapo. After being incorporated into Seoul in 1963, it was separated from Gangnam-gu in 1979 to become Gangdong-gu. Subsequently, Songpa-gu was established as a separate district in 1988.

People & Stories

Duncheon-dong derives its name from the pen name of Lee Jip (李集), a scholar from the late Goryeo period. Lee Jip was renowned for his profound filial piety; a legend known as the ‘Gukcheongsa Well’ tells how he cured his father’s boil using a golden carp from the well water he obtained after offering a hundred-day prayer at Gukcheongsa Temple. This well, associated with his filial devotion, is said to still remain within Namhansanseong Fortress today. In Amsa-dong, traces remain of Neolithic people who lived along the riverbank, catching fish and making comb-patterned pottery, demonstrating that Seoul has been inhabited since prehistoric times. In addition, the 'Sangyeo Seonso' songs sung during funeral processions and the 'Bama Taryeong' songs from the 'Dalgujil Nori' folk game have been passed down through the generations.
