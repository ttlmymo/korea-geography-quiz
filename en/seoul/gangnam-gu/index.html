Gangnam-gu, Seoul

About Gangnam-gu

An autonomous district located east of the Han River in Seoul, it is a major commercial and business hub centered around Teheran-ro. It is home to COEX and Bongeunsa Temple, and is also well known as an area with a strong emphasis on education.

History of Gangnam-gu

Situated on the northwestern slopes of Mount Guryong and Mount Daemo, as well as on the hills and plains north and south of Yangjae Stream, the area has been inhabited since the Paleolithic Era, and Bronze Age settlement sites have been confirmed in Yeoksam-dong. During the Three Kingdoms period, it was a battleground for the struggle over the Han River basin, and traces of the Samseong-dong earthen fortress and Daemo Mountain Fortress remain. During the Joseon Dynasty, it was a rural area along the Han River belonging to Eonju-myeon and Daewang-myeon of Gwangju-bu; the freshwater crabs from Apgujeong were famous as tribute items to the royal court. It was incorporated into Seoul in 1963, and in 1975, Gangnam District was newly established from Seongdong District; subsequently, Gangdong District and Seocho District were successively separated from it. Following the 1970 South Seoul Development Plan and the opening of the Hannam Bridge and the Gyeongbu Expressway, the area experienced rapid growth and has become one of Seoul’s leading urban centers today; as a result, the term 'Yeongdong,' derived from its old name, is still used in some contexts.

People & Stories

Bongeunsa Temple in Samseong-dong is said to have been founded during the Silla period and played an important role as the royal temple of the Joseon dynasty. Its front courtyard is called ‘Seunggwa-pyeong’ (Monk Examination Ground) because, during the reign of King Myeongjong, Monk Bowo presided over the monk examination there with the support of Queen Munjeong, attracting thousands of monks from across the country. There is also the ‘Bo-eun-dan Folklore,’ which tells of Hong Sun-eon, an interpreter from Cheongdam-dong, who helped a woman in distress in China; that woman later became the wife of a high-ranking Ming official and provided significant assistance during the Imjin War. In addition, there are tales such as the story of the Stele of King Taejong at Heonreung, which is said to sweat when the nation faces calamity, and the legend of Maljuk-geori, which originated from King Injo’s escape.
