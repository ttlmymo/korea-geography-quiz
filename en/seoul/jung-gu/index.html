Jung-gu, Seoul

중구 · 74 neighborhoods · Learn Korean administrative divisions with maps

About Jung-gu

Located south of Cheonggyecheon Stream and on the northern slopes of Namsan Mountain, this autonomous district spans half of the Seoul Basin and is the smallest in area among the city’s 25 districts. It is Seoul’s premier central business district, with a high concentration of financial, commercial, media, and printing industries in the Myeong-dong, Eulji-ro, Sogong-dong, and Chungmu-ro areas, and is a place where commerce and history coexist, featuring landmarks such as Namdaemun Market, Namsan, and Deoksugung Palace.

History of Jung-gu

In ancient times, this area was part of the Mahan Confederacy and early Baekje. After a period of rivalry among the Three Kingdoms, it became part of Hanyang County during the Unified Silla period and belonged to Namgyeong during the Goryeo Dynasty, with this area serving as the heart of Namgyeong. During the Joseon Dynasty, it comprised the entire southern region and part of the western region of Hanseongbu, forming Namchon (South Village) within the capital walls, where the residences of the royal family and high-ranking officials, as well as shrines and royal tombs, were located. After the opening of the ports, Japanese commercial districts and economic exploitation institutions such as the Joseon Bank and the Oriental Development Company were established in the Namdaemun, Myeong-dong, and Chungmu-ro areas, marking the beginning of Seoul’s financial district. In 1943, with the implementation of the district system, the area within the city walls was divided into Jongno-gu and Jung-gu, marking the establishment of Jung-gu. Overcoming the damage caused by the Liberation and the Korean War, the area underwent urban redevelopment and has established itself as the central hub of Seoul’s political, economic, and cultural life today.

People & Stories

Jung-gu is home to a concentration of cultural heritage sites spanning the history of the Joseon Dynasty and the modern era, including Sungnyemun (Namdaemun)—the main gate of the Joseon capital and a National Treasure—as well as Deoksugung Palace, Hwangudan, Myeongdong Cathedral, Yakhyeon Cathedral, the old Seoul Station, and the Bank of Korea headquarters. In the Namsangol Hanok Village in Pil-dong, traditional houses from the late 19th century—including the residence of Master Carpenter Lee Seung-eop—have been relocated to showcase the lifestyle of old Seoul, while in Yejang-dong on Namsan Mountain, the site of the Mokmyeok Mountain Beacon Tower, where beacon signals from Hamgyeong Province were received, still remains. In addition, various legends about place names are passed down, such as the legend of Beotigi-goe, whose name is said to have originated from the patrolmen shouting 'Beon-do' due to the high number of thieves in the area.
