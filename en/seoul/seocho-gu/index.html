About Seocho-gu

Located in southern Seoul, this autonomous district faces the Han River with Mount Cheonggye and Mount Umyeon as its backdrop. Along with Gangnam, it is one of Seoul’s leading residential and business hubs. Home to the Seoul Arts Center, the National Library of Korea, the Supreme Court, and the Supreme Prosecutors’ Office, it serves as a center for culture and the judiciary; it is also the site of Heonneung and Inneung, royal tombs from the Joseon Dynasty.

History of Seocho-gu

During the Three Kingdoms period, the area was caught in the power struggle among Baekje, Goguryeo, and Silla over the Han River basin. During the Unified Silla period, it was part of Yuljin County; during the Joseon Dynasty, it was a rural area south of the capital, belonging to Eonju-myeon in Gwangju County and Gwacheon County. As it lay on the route connecting the capital to the Samnam region, a post station village known as ‘Maljuk-geori’ developed in the area now known as Yangjae-dong. After being incorporated into Seoul in 1963, it became part of Gangnam-gu in 1975. In 1988, 11 statutory neighborhoods—including Seocho, Jamwon, Banpo, Bangbae, and Yangjae-dong—were separated from Gangnam-gu to form the newly established Seocho-gu. Triggered by the Yeongdong Land Readjustment Project that began in 1968, the area underwent rapid urbanization and developed into the planned city it is today.

People & Stories

In Naegok-dong, Heon-neung—the tomb of King Taejong and Queen Wongyeong—and In-neung—the tomb of King Sunjo and Queen Sunwon—stand side by side, serving as important heritage sites that illustrate the burial rites of the Joseon royal family. The Seoul Arts Center, located at the foot of Umyeon Mountain, features an opera house modeled after a scholar’s hat and a fan-shaped concert hall, forming a complex cultural and arts space alongside the National Center for Korean Traditional Performing Arts and the Korea National University of Arts. Additionally, in Banpo-dong, a legend is passed down about Beolbawi Rock and Malmudeum—a story in which a swarm of bees emerged to punish a soldier after he killed a baby whose umbilical cord had been cut with a reed.
