Songpa-gu, Seoul

About Songpa-gu

Located in southeastern Seoul on the south bank of the Han River, this autonomous district is a historic area that served as the center of Hanseong Baekje, where the Baekje Kingdom first established its capital. Today, it has grown into a commercial and cultural hub of southeastern Seoul, represented by the Olympic facilities in the Jamsil area, Lotte World, and Seokchon Lake.

History of Songpa-gu

People have lived here since ancient times, as evidenced by Paleolithic and Bronze Age artifacts unearthed in the Garak-dong and Munjeong-dong areas. Around the turn of the Common Era, Baekje established its capital in this region, laying the foundation for 500 years of Hanseong Baekje centered on the Pungnap and Mongchon earthen fortresses. During the Joseon Dynasty, the area belonged to Jungdae-myeon in Gwangju-bu, and the Songpa Market, centered around Songpa Ferry, flourished as a major marketplace attracting merchants from across the country. In 1963, the area was incorporated into Seoul; after passing through Seongdong-gu, Gangnam-gu, and Gangdong-gu, it was separated from Gangdong-gu in 1988, and Songpa-gu was established. In the 1970s, the comprehensive development of the Jamsil District connected Jamsil Island—which had been an island in the river—to the mainland, and the construction of large-scale apartment complexes gave the area its current appearance.

People & Stories

The Pungnap Earthen Fortress and Mongchon Earthen Fortress in Pungnap-dong and Bangi-dong, along with the burial mounds in Seokchon-dong and Bangi-dong, are precious heritage sites that reveal traces of the royal family and the capital’s residents during the Hanseong Baekje period, bearing witness to Seoul’s 2,000-year history. In Seokchon-dong, the Samjeondo Stele—erected under pressure from the Qing Dynasty following the Manchu Invasion of 1636—remains, recounting the painful history of King Injo’s surrender to the Qing Emperor Taizong. Furthermore, the Songpa Sandae Nori and Songpa Bridge-Stepping rituals, which were once performed at the thriving Songpa Market centered around Songpa Ferry, have been passed down to this day, carrying on the lively culture of the old marketplace.
