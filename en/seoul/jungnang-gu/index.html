Jungnang-gu, Seoul

About Jungnang-gu

Located in northeastern Seoul, this autonomous district is situated on a terrain that slopes from east to west, with the Jungnang Stream flowing along its edge and backed by a mountain range stretching from Mount Bulam to Mount Acha. It is a residential area rich in history spanning from prehistoric times to the modern era, home to the Paleolithic site in Myeonmok-dong, the Goguryeo fortresses on Mount Bonghwa and Mount Manguri, and Manguri History and Culture Park, where prominent figures from modern and contemporary history are laid to rest.

History of Jungnang-gu

As evidenced by the Myeonmok-dong Paleolithic site—the only one excavated in the Han River basin in Seoul—and the Bronze Age sites in Sangbong-dong and on Mount Bonghwa, this area has been inhabited since prehistoric times and is also considered a leading candidate for the location of the Hapuk Wirye Fortress. During the Three Kingdoms period, it was a border region where the three kingdoms vied for control—so much so that Goguryeo fortresses were constructed in the areas around Bonghwasan, Mangurisan, and Yongmabong. During the Joseon Dynasty, it belonged to Manguriri-myeon and Goyangju-myeon in Yangju, serving as a key junction on the royal procession route to Geonwonneung and as the site of a national ranch. In 1963, it was incorporated into Seoul and placed under the jurisdiction of the Manguri Branch Office of Dongdaemun-gu; in 1988, this area was separated to form the newly established Jungnang-gu. As urbanization progressed in this former fruit-farming region, factories and the Manguri-ro commercial district emerged, and since the 1990s, the development of new residential areas has transformed it into a leading residential district in northeastern Seoul.

People & Stories

Manguri History and Culture Park, which originated from the Manguri Public Cemetery, is the final resting place of various modern and contemporary figures, including Bang Jeong-hwan, a pioneer of the children’s movement, and Oh Se-chang and Han Yong-un, who were among the 33 National Representatives. At its entrance stands the Monument to the 13-Province Righteous Army, commemorating the 1907 Jeongmi Righteous Army’s advance on Seoul, and at the summit of Bonghwasan Mountain, the site of the beacon tower has been restored, allowing the Bonghwasan Dang-gut ritual to continue as a Seoul Intangible Cultural Heritage. Additionally, the area is home to the legend of 'Jung-nang,' whose name is said to derive from a story of a filial daughter who disguised herself as a man to take the place of her blind father, who had been conscripted into forced labor, as well as the legend of the Meokgol Pear, which is said to have been cultivated by Wang Bang-yeon, who escorted King Danjong.
