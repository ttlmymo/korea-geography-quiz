About Mapo-gu

Located west of Seoul along the lower reaches of the Han River, this autonomous district was once a logistics hub for the Mapo and Seogang ferry crossings, where grain and seafood from the Samnam region converged during the Joseon Dynasty. Today, it has grown into a hub for the cultural and media industries, epitomized by Hongdae and Sangam Digital Media City.

History of Mapo-gu

During the Three Kingdoms period, it was caught in the power struggle among Baekje, Goguryeo, and Silla over the Han River basin. During the Joseon Dynasty, it belonged to the Yongsan-bang and Seogang-bang districts—located ten ri outside the capital walls—and flourished as a port along the Han River. The Mapo, Seogang, and Yanghwa ferry crossings served as collection points for grain taxes, seafood, and various goods coming from the Three Southern Provinces and the West Sea, forming the core of logistics on the Han River. In 1944, Mapo-gu was established by separating parts of Seodaemun-gu and Yongsan-gu, and after several boundary adjustments, it came to include Seongsan and Sangam-dong. Although the old port culture faded as Ganghwa Bay was blocked off and land transportation developed, the area has reemerged as a media and cultural hub alongside the development of Sangam-dong.

People & Stories

Jamdubong Hill along the Han River in Hapjeong-dong is where countless Catholic believers were martyred during the Byeongin Persecution; it is known as ‘Jeoldu-san’ (Mount Beheading) and is now home to a martyrs’ shrine and a memorial hall. In Dohwa-dong, the neighborhood takes its name from the legend of Dohwa Nangja, which is said to have originated from a peach tree planted by a father who longed for his daughter—who had become the daughter-in-law of the Jade Emperor. Additionally, in Tojeong-dong, the site of the birthplace of Tojeong Lee Ji-ham—who is said to have authored Tojeong Bigyeol—remains, demonstrating that Mapo is a region steeped in a long history of scholarship and stories.
