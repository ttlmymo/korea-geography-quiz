About Gangseo-gu

Located on the Gimpo Plain along the lower reaches of the Han River at the western edge of Seoul, this autonomous district serves as the gateway to Seoul, home to Gimpo International Airport. The Hwagok-dong and Magok-dong areas have undergone large-scale development and grown into residential and business districts, while historic cultural heritage sites such as Yangcheon Hyanggyo and Heoga Rock remain preserved.

History of Gangseo-gu

The former name of Gangseo-gu was ‘Jechapawi’ (齊次巴衣), meaning ‘the rock where rituals are performed,’ which is said to have originated from Heoga Rock on the banks of the Han River. During the Three Kingdoms period, the area was ruled alternately by Baekje and Goguryeo, and during the Unified Silla period, it became Gimpo County, belonging to the Gimpo and Yangcheon regions for a long time. In 1963, it was incorporated into Seoul from Gimpo County, Gyeonggi Province, and became part of Yeongdeungpo-gu, before being separated and established as Gangseo-gu in 1977. Later, upon the completion of the Mok-dong New Town development, the areas of Mok-dong, Sinjeong-dong, and Sinwol-dong were re-divided in 1988 to establish Yangcheon District.

People & Stories

The Heoga Rock in Gayang-dong is a natural rock cave said to be the birthplace of the Heo clan of Yangcheon. It is said that Heo Jun, the renowned physician who authored the Donguibogam, wrote his book here and passed away at this site. At the foot of Gungsan Mountain stands Yangcheon Hyanggyo, the only Confucian school in Seoul, where the Seokjeon Daeje ritual is held in spring and fall to carry on the spirit of the ancient scholars. There is also a legend that General Kim Mal-son drove away the ‘Ghost Rock,’ which had been threatening the village, by shooting an arrow at it, and that he built Yeongbyeokjeong Pavilion nearby.
