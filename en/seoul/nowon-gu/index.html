About Nowon-gu

Located at the northeastern tip of Seoul, this is a large residential district where the Jungnang Stream flows against the backdrop of Mount Surak and Mount Bulam. Home to large, planned apartment complexes in the Sanggye and Junggye-dong areas, as well as several universities, the Taereung National Training Center, and Taereung itself, this district harmoniously blends education, sports, and the history of the Joseon royal tombs.

History of Nowon-gu

In ancient times, this was a border region contested by the Three Kingdoms. Following King Jangsu of Goguryeo’s southward expansion, it became part of Maeseong County and later became Naeso County during the Unified Silla period. During the Joseon Dynasty, it belonged to Nowon-myeon in Yangju; ‘Nowon (蘆原)’ means ‘a field overgrown with reeds,’ a name derived from the area’s ancient topography, and there was a large market called Nowonjeon, located at a key junction on the road to Hamgyeong Province. In 1963, it was incorporated into Seoul; after briefly being part of Seongbuk-gu, it became part of Dobong-gu in 1973. As the population grew explosively, the areas of Sanggye, Junggye, Hagye, and Wolgye-dong were separated from Dobong-gu in 1988 to establish the new Nowon-gu. Subsequently, with the development of the new Sanggye and Junggye residential districts, it has established itself as a leading residential area in northeastern Seoul.

People & Stories

Gongneung-dong is home to Tae-neung, the tomb of Queen Munjeong of the Joseon Dynasty, and Gangneung, the tomb of King Myeongjong, which remain important heritage sites showcasing the Joseon royal tomb system. The area around Taereung is widely known as an iconic site for Korean sports, home to the Taereung National Training Center—once the cradle of national athletes—as well as the International Shooting Range and Ice Rink. Additionally, the area is home to the touching legend of a hunter and his son associated with the name of Suraksan Mountain, and the legend of Bulamsan Mountain, which is said to have turned away after failing to become Namsan Mountain.
