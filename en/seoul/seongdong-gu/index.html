Seongdong-gu, Seoul

About Seongdong-gu

This autonomous district is located in the Wangsimni area, east of downtown Seoul, where the Cheonggyecheon and Jungnangcheon streams converge and flow into the Han River. Once home to Salgoteui Valley—a royal hunting ground during the Joseon Dynasty—and horse pastures, it is now emerging as a new cultural hotspot in Seoul, best known for the handmade shoe district and café district in Seongsu-dong.

History of Seongdong-gu

The discovery of comb-patterned pottery and Bronze Age artifacts at the foot of Eungbong Mountain in Oksoo-dong along the Han River demonstrates that people have lived here since the Neolithic Age. During the Three Kingdoms Period, it served as a strategic stronghold over which the three kingdoms vied for control of the Han River basin. During the Joseon Dynasty, it belonged to the Inchang-bang and Dumo-bang districts within the 'Seongjeo Sipri' (Ten-Li Perimeter) of Hanseongbu. It was the eastern outskirts of the capital, home to Dumo-po—where goods from the upper Han River were gathered—Salgoteui-beol, the king’s hunting grounds, and Dokseodang, a school for training officials. Seongdong-gu was established in 1943 as part of a municipal reorganization. It took on its current form in 1975 when the area south of the Han River was separated to form Gangnam-gu, and again in 1995 when the area east of Dongil-ro was separated to form Gwangjin-gu. Since the Japanese colonial period, it has developed into a key transportation hub and semi-industrial area centered around Wangsimni Station.

People & Stories

It is said that the name Wangsimni originates from a legend in which Great Monk Muhak, while searching for a site to establish the capital, was told by an old man to ‘go ten ri further’ (往十里). Salgote Bridge, which connects the Sagun-dong and Seongsu-dong areas, is a stone bridge built in the early Joseon Dynasty. It is designated as a National Treasure and is associated with a legend in which King Taejo, Yi Seong-gye, shot an arrow at King Taejong, but it struck a pillar; he interpreted this as a sign of divine will. Additionally, Anjeongsa Temple in Hawangsimni-dong is said to have been founded during the Silla Dynasty and later rebuilt by Venerable Muhak.
