Seongbuk-gu, Seoul

성북구 · 39 neighborhoods · Learn Korean administrative divisions with maps

About Seongbuk-gu

This autonomous district is situated on the rolling hills north of Seoul’s city walls, where the foothills of Mount Bukhansan extend into Mount Gaewun. It is an area where the historic cultural and arts village of Seongbuk-dong blends with the university district, and where traces of modern and contemporary culture—such as the Gansong Art Museum and Simu-jang—remain deeply embedded.

History of Seongbuk-gu

During the Three Kingdoms period, it was a border region where the three kingdoms vied for control of the Han River basin. In the Joseon Dynasty, it belonged to the Sungshin-bang and Inchang-bang districts within the 'Seongjeo Sipri' (Ten Li Around the Capital) of Hanseongbu, serving as the northern gateway to the capital. Samseonpyeong in Seongbuk-dong was home to a military training ground and the Bukchang gate of the Royal Guard Command, and it also served as a key thoroughfare for merchants traveling along the northeastern arterial road that passed through Hyehwa Gate and led to Wonsan. In 1949, Seongbuk-gu was established by merging part of Dongdaemun-gu with the former Sungin-myeon area of Goyang County; subsequently, in 1973, Dobong-gu was separated from it, and after several boundary adjustments, the district has evolved into its current form. In modern times, as various universities and writers and painters began to gather here, the area established itself as a leading center of education and culture in Seoul.

People & Stories

The Gansong Art Museum in Seongbuk-dong houses Korean cultural treasures that Jeon Hyung-pil preserved by spending his own fortune during the Japanese colonial period; it is known to hold several National Treasures, including the Hunminjeongeum Haerye (Explanation of the Proper Sounds for the Instruction of the People). It is said that Simu-jang, where Manhae Han Yong-un spent his final years, was deliberately built facing north to turn his back on the Japanese Government-General of Korea, and it remains a place that exemplifies his unwavering integrity. Additionally, local legends are passed down, such as the story of Angamnae Bridge, which was built in front of the village after a vegetable vendor named An Gam pleaded for a bridge to be constructed.
